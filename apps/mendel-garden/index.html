4 · Virtual CRISPR lesson concept only — no procedures

Mendel's crosses shuffle whole decks of genes and let chance deal. Gene editing — CRISPR is the famous tool — works more like molecular scissors with an address: it can change one chosen trait directly. Same destination as breeding, very different road. This lesson teaches the idea and its consequences; it contains no methods, on purpose.

goal: a blight-resistant potato (resistance 8/10, starting from 1/10). walk both roads:

  • Precision is not omniscience. Editing targets one address, but genomes are crowded: edits can land slightly off-target, and a "one-trait" change can ripple. Real programs screen many candidate plants and discard most — that screening, not the cutting, is where the care lives.
  • Most traits still aren't switches. Blight resistance can involve several genes; drought tolerance, dozens. Editing doesn't repeal polygenic complexity — the same humility this garden's peas taught.
  • The ethics are about people. Who owns an edited seed — the farmer or the patent holder? Should edited food be labeled so eaters can choose? Different countries answer differently, and both questions are older than CRISPR: they started with the first patented hybrid.
  • Why there are no procedures here. Understanding what a tool does, what it costs, and who it affects is citizenship. Performing it belongs in supervised labs with oversight boards. This page deliberately teaches the first and not the second.

5 · The ethics, said out loud

  • "Dominant" does not mean better. It is bookkeeping about which copy shows, nothing more. White flowers are not defective purple ones.
  • Most human traits are not pea traits. Height, eye color, and almost everything interesting involve many genes plus environment. One-gene charts of complex human traits are almost always wrong — and historically, dangerous.
  • The dangerous history has a name. Eugenics dressed itself in exactly these diagrams to rank human beings. The math never supported it, and the movement's legacy is coercion and harm. Knowing the real math is a defense against its misuse.
  • This page is arithmetic, on purpose. No lab steps, no modification techniques — inheritance probability is the same fair coin as any dice game, and that is all a beginner needs.
