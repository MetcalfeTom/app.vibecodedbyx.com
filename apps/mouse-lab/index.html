What the numbers mean

Drift up/down adds up every vertical move while you rock sideways, in degrees of view turn (0.12° per pixel, the way a game camera turns) and pixels. Every degree except the tilt's slope is view turn, not the angle of the line, so it can go past 90°. A steady hand lands near zero; a browser or driver that adds its own vertical movement makes the view creep one way, run after run.

Wander is how far the view swung up and down during the run, top to bottom: a view that goes 10° up and comes back ends level but wandered 10°.

Tilt is how the verdict is judged. It puts a box around the whole line after the warm-up: the box's rise (the line's height, top to bottom) over its run (all your sideways travel laid end to end). Its slope is the angle of the box's diagonal, atan(rise / run), always between 0 and 90°. Under 1% tilt is Level, 1 to 3% is slight, 3% or more is noticeable, so a flat line that wobbles a few pixels is Level. The chart shows the box once a run ends.

Free cursor is the control run: it measures your own hand and remembers its tilt. Pointer lock is then judged on the tilt it adds beyond your hand.

Two rules can overrule the box, because a long run can make even a big slope a small tilt. The end rule: the view ended more than 5° off, or 10° or more for noticeable. The swing rule: the view swung 10° or more top to bottom, beyond what your hand does. In pointer lock, huge jumps and disagreeing event streams overrule it too. The verdict always says which rule decided.

Lean splits that drift by direction: if moving right pushes the view up and moving left pushes it up too, it isn't your hand.

Warm-up: the first 2 s of every run (up to 5 s, until your moves turn sideways) aren't counted, so getting the mouse into place doesn't skew anything. The chart shows them grey.

Spikes are single reports far bigger than your usual move: the view jumps somewhere you never pointed. Only huge ones (over 150 px and 10 times your usual move) count against pointer lock; with a free cursor they are normal. Stalls are gaps of 60 ms or more with no reports while you keep moving.

Pointer lock, raw asks the browser to skip your system's mouse acceleration. Pointer lock isn't available on phones and some browsers.
