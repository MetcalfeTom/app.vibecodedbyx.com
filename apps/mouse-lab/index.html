What the numbers mean

Drift up/down adds up every vertical move while you rock sideways, in degrees of view (0.12° per pixel) and pixels. A steady hand lands near zero; a browser or driver that adds its own vertical movement makes the view creep one way, run after run.

Wander is how far the view swung up and down during the run, top to bottom: a view that goes 10° up and comes back ends level but wandered 10°.

Tilt is the main measure: a box around the whole line, its rise (the wander) over its run (all your sideways travel laid end to end). A mostly straight line with a little jitter has a small rise over a long run, so it reads as level.

Free cursor is the control run: it measures your own hand's tilt (level up to 6%) and remembers it. Pointer lock is then judged only on what it adds beyond your hand, in degrees of view: level up to 3°, noticeable from 10°. Without a baseline, pointer lock is judged on tilt alone (level up to 3%).

Lean splits that drift by direction: if moving right pushes the view up and moving left pushes it up too, it isn't your hand.

Warm-up: the first 2 s of every run (up to 5 s, until your moves turn sideways) aren't counted, so getting the mouse into place doesn't skew anything. The chart shows them grey.

Spikes are single reports far bigger than your usual move: the view jumps somewhere you never pointed. Only huge ones (over 150 px and 10 times your usual move) count against pointer lock; with a free cursor they are normal. Stalls are gaps of 60 ms or more with no reports while you keep moving.

Pointer lock, raw asks the browser to skip your system's mouse acceleration. Pointer lock isn't available on phones and some browsers.
