Sources, as named above

  • WSDA / USDA APHIS outreach materials (2020–2021) — Washington State Department of Agriculture and USDA fact sheets on Vespa mandarinia repeatedly state flight speeds "up to about 20 mph"; some agency communications say 25. An agency talking point, not a peer-reviewed measurement — hence the label.
  • Kennedy, Ford, Poidatz, Thiéry & Osborne (2018), Communications Biology — "Searching for nests of the invasive Asian hornet using radio-telemetry": tracked Vespa velutina workers carrying transmitters; observed flights were on the order of a few metres per second, and the transmitter load plausibly slowed them — the label says so.
  • General entomology references (e.g., popular summaries of Vespa crabro) — the oft-quoted "about 13–14 mph" for the European hornet traces to older secondary literature; a commonly cited figure with thin primary sourcing, labelled accordingly.
  • Winston, "The Biology of the Honey Bee" (1987) and standard apiology references — honeybee cruising flight around 6–8 m/s is one of the best-established insect flight figures; included as the calibration animal.
