what fuzz does to fridge food, day by day — look, decide, never taste

day 0

three rules, no exceptions

  • Eyes only. Never taste to check, and don't rely on sniffing — mold toxins don't announce themselves.
  • When in doubt, throw it out. A verdict you're unsure about is already a verdict.
  • Soft and wet means the whole item goes. The fuzzy dot is only the fruiting tip — invisible roots run deep through moist food.

🎮 the neighbors game

Real fridge science: ethylene is a ripening hormone gas — apples and bananas breathe it out, berries and tomatoes listen and hurry. And mold spreads by touch. Arrange six foods on the shelf (two slots stay empty — spacing is a strategy), then run the week and see who survives.

the week's verdicts — eyes only, as always

    🎮 level 2: the leaky package

    Someone left raw chicken on an upper shelf. Real rule, USDA FSIS by name: raw meat belongs on the bottom shelf, in a tray, so juices can't reach anything below. Juices carry bacteria that no look can clear — this level is about containment, not chance. Move the package, place your two absorbent pads (a pad in the meat's own cell acts as its tray), then run five days.

    the spill is over — now clean up SAFELY, in order

    Tap the steps in the order you'd do them. Bacteria from raw juices are invisible — cleanup is about method, never about how surfaces look or smell.

    🧺 shelf lifted out — now scrub every corner

    Shelves come OUT for washing — that's what removable means. Corners collect grime, so they take two passes. And some spores hide where everything looks clean: you find them by washing the whole surface, never by inspection.

    containment report

      🗺 the fridge campaign

      Seven days, one shelf, three mystery leftovers with no labels and unknown pasts. Each day you take one manual snapshot — a deliberate look, nothing automatic — and the fridge offers clues: a whiff, fuzz at a seam, a sticky ring. Clues only ever say "toss sooner." No clue can ever say "safe" — and unlabeled leftovers past four days are a toss with no clues needed (the real leftovers rule).

        🌱 the compost question

        decided AFTER the discard decision · a waste question, never a safety question

        Mold in a compost pile isn't a problem — it's the job description. Decomposer fungi are exactly what composting is. The only reason some foods stay out of home bins is pests and smell, not the mold itself; many municipal food-waste programs accept them all.
