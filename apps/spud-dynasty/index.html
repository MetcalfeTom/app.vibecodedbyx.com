what the soil teaches

  • The monoculture trap is real. Ireland, 1845: nearly one variety — the Lumper — fed a nation, and late blight (a fungus-like oomycete) destroyed it in three seasons. Plant all four slots with one variety here and watch disease pressure climb the same way.
  • Breeding is trade-offs, not upgrades. Offspring average their parents plus a little luck, and no potato maxes every trait — pushing yield usually costs resilience somewhere. That tension is the whole craft.
  • Diversity is the strategy. Different strengths in different slots means something survives every season. Real gene banks keep thousands of potato varieties for exactly this reason.
  • Nothing here is a protocol. This is selective breeding — pick two parents, plant the child — as practiced since the Andes domesticated the potato. No lab, no gene editing, just choice and chance.
