    honest physics

    REALGravity here is true inverse-square (F ∝ GM/r²). Ellipses, circular orbits at v=√(GM/r), and escape at √2× that all emerge — nothing is scripted.

    CHAOS MODETwo bodies are clockwork — integrable, forever predictable. Flip on the rogue moon and it's a restricted three-body problem: genuinely chaotic. The twin satellites launch 0.1% apart and their separation roughly doubles again and again — sensitive dependence, the fingerprint of chaos. (Orbital-element labels are suspended in this mode; those formulas assume two bodies. The moon's pull is slightly softened so slingshots don't explode the integrator — the chaos is real, the taming is admitted.)

    SIMPLIFIEDTwo dimensions; the planet never moves or spins; no atmosphere, no drag, no third bodies unless you summon the rogue moon; units are game units, not SI; and the integrator (small-step semi-implicit Euler) drifts slightly over many orbits — real missions use better math and still correct course.

    sources & honesty

    DATAPlanetary a, e, T, mean orbital velocity, mass and radius: NASA NSSDCA Planetary Fact Sheet, D.R. Williams, NASA Goddard (values rounded; search "NSSDCA planetary fact sheet" — no link, by house rules).
    MATHWhat-ifs use Kepler's third law T′=√(a′³/M☉′) with T in years, a in AU, mass in Suns; v′≈29.78·√(M☉′/a′) km/s is the circular approximation — real orbits carry the eccentricities in the table.
    ROTATION & MOONSidereal rotation periods (hours, minus = retrograde) and lunar elements (a=384,400 km · T=27.32 d sidereal · e=0.055) also from the NSSDCA fact sheets. The Moon is tidally locked: its day equals its month. Eclipse note: this orrery is 2D and coplanar, so Sun–Earth–Moon alignments recur every synodic pass — the real Moon's orbit tilts ~5.1°, so true eclipses only happen when alignment meets a node crossing. Our syzygies are the geometry lesson, not an eclipse calendar (for the real thing, see the Umbra app).
    SIZE & HABITABILITYThe size dial scales radius ×s with mass ∝ s³ (same density), so surface gravity and escape velocity both scale ×s. The warning thresholds — thin-air risk below ~0.5 Earth sizes, the rocky-to-gaseous transition near ~1.6 Earth radii — follow the broad strokes of exoplanet statistics (e.g. Rogers 2015 and NASA exoplanet survey summaries; cited by name, no links). Size does not change the orbit: to first order Kepler only asks about the Sun.
    DRAWN, NOT TRUEOrrery distances are log-compressed and planet dots are wildly oversized — at true scale this canvas would be one lonely pixel of Sun. Orbits are drawn circular at mean distance; e is listed, not painted. The Moon's display ring is exaggerated ~40× or it would hide inside Earth's dot; its 27.32-day rhythm is real.
