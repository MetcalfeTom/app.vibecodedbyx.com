How the number works

0° is solar noon at the pin: the sun crosses your meridian and is as high as it gets today. The number grows by 15° an hour as the Earth turns, so 90° is about six in the evening, 180° is solar midnight with the sun right under your feet, and 270° is about six in the morning. One degree is four minutes of sun time.

Astronomers call this the sun's hour angle. It ignores time zones and daylight saving, so two friends a few hundred kilometres apart read different numbers at the same moment. It also wobbles by up to 16 minutes (4°) against clock time over the year, because Earth's orbit is an ellipse and its axis is tilted. That wobble is the equation of time, shown in the readouts.

The gold arc is when the sun is up, the purple bands are twilight. Near the poles the arc can fill the whole circle (midnight sun) or vanish (polar night).
