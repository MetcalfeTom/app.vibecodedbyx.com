      Dossier 2026-1005 · Issoire, Puy-de-Dôme

      The bronze cover is gone.

      In the night of 4 to 5 October 2026, the bronze cover Salvador Dalí sculpted for the book L'Apocalypse de Saint Jean was stolen from a contemporary art centre in Issoire, France.

      • The cover alone weighs about 150 kg, set with gold, precious stones and seashells.
      • The whole book: 210 kg, 150 m long unfolded, 150 parchments, 95,000 hand-written letters.
      • Seven painters (Dalí, Foujita, Georges Mathieu…) and seven writers (Cocteau, Jean Rostand, Cioran, Giono, Ernst Jünger…) worked on it for publisher Joseph Forêt, from 1958.
      • Nobody can explain how something so heavy left unnoticed.

      There is no picture of it here. So help the investigators: witnesses each remember a few details. Rebuild the cover on the empty plinth to match, and the curator will tell you if it fits the testimony.

      The museum and the investigation
      • The book was kept at the Centre C. Karoutzos, a private art centre in Issoire that opened in 2010.
      • Its founder, collector Christian Karoutzos, runs a restoration company in Issoire. According to reports, he bought the book in Japan, brought it to France in 2003, and says it took him ten years to pay for it.
      • From 1961 to 1972 the book toured in about 150 exhibitions. When it was made it was valued at 2 million francs and called the most expensive book in the world.
      • The parchment pages were kept elsewhere and were not reported stolen.
      • The gendarmerie's research section in Clermont-Ferrand is investigating. Experts fear the cover could be broken up for its gold and stones.

      Facts: news reports, 6 Oct 2026. The witnesses in the game are made up, and what you build is your own homage, not the real cover.

        Ten famous art heists and one open case

        1. Case open

          2026

          Issoire · Puy-de-Dôme

          The Apocalypse cover

          About 150 kg of bronze, gold, precious stones and seashells, gone in one night. Nobody can yet explain how it left unnoticed.

        2. 1911

          Paris · the Louvre

          The Mona Lisa

          Vincenzo Peruggia walked out with it under his smock. It turned up in Florence in 1913.

        3. 1934

          Ghent

          The Just Judges

          A panel known as "The Just Judges" was stolen. It has never been found.

        4. Marci's pick

          1981

          Den Bosch

          De Rolstoelrovers (the wheelchair robbers)

          Two young men from Rosmalen and Den Bosch: one in a wheelchair, the other pushing it. With a homemade extendable fishing rod with a loop on the end, they lifted saints' statues into a bag hanging on the wheelchair. St Barbara, from around 1520, came down from 4 metres up.

          They went on to churches in Hasselt, Sint-Truiden and Ham (Oostham). In October 1982 a young police officer in Rosmalen stopped a car driving the wrong way and found fake passports and a rental contract for a garage box.

          Inside the garage: more than 50 statues, set out like a little museum, some of them even restored. None were ever sold. They said they simply found them beautiful.

          A 2026 Dutch podcast, De Rolstoelrovers, tells the whole story.

        5. 1990

          Boston · Gardner Museum

          Thirteen works

          Men dressed as police officers took 13 works, Vermeer's The Concert among them. Still unsolved: the empty frames still hang.

        6. 1994

          Oslo

          The Scream

          The thieves left a note thanking the museum for its poor security. The painting was back the same year.

        7. 2000

          Stockholm

          A Rembrandt and two Renoirs

          The getaway was by speedboat. All three were recovered by 2005.

        8. 2002

          Amsterdam · Van Gogh Museum

          Two Van Goghs

          In through the roof with a ladder, out with two paintings. They were found in Italy in 2016.

        9. 2012

          Rotterdam · Kunsthal

          Seven paintings

          Picasso, Matisse, Monet and Gauguin among them, taken at night in a matter of minutes.

        10. 2019

          Dresden · Green Vault

          18th-century jewels

          Most of them were recovered years later.

        11. 2025

          Paris · the Louvre

          The crown jewels

          Taken from the Galerie d'Apollon, with a furniture lift up to a window. One crown was dropped outside.
