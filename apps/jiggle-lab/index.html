🧪 what am I actually seeing?

This is the ball-and-spring cartoon that chemists really do use for intuition — atoms are soft balls, bonds are springs following Hooke's law (pull twice as far, feel twice the force), and temperature is nothing more mysterious than how fast everything jiggles on average.

Cold: springs win, atoms hold a tidy lattice — that's solid behavior. Warm: jiggling loosens the order but bonds mostly hold — melting. Hot: jiggling beats the springs, bonds stretch past their limit and snap — things fly apart like a gas. Bond color shows strain: green is relaxed, red is about to give.

The water demo is the good stuff: each molecule holds together with strong springs (drawn as coils), while weak dashed bonds knit separate molecules to each other — a cartoon of hydrogen bonding. Warm it and the weak bonds give up first: that's why ice melts long before the molecules themselves break. Cool the steam back down and a faint long-range pull (our cartoon of van-der-Waals attraction) drifts the molecules together until weak bonds re-form — condensation, then freezing, for free, from nothing but springs and one gentle pull.

Honesty note: real molecules obey quantum mechanics and this toy does not — no electrons, no light, no real chemistry. It's the classical springs-only model, which is exactly why it's safe to melt everything.
