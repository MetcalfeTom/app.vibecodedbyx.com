The carousel

The Moon is drawn the way it hangs in your sky: lit side toward the Sun, turned for your latitude, rocking slightly as its face wobbles. Its size is exaggerated so you can see it, so near the Sun it shrinks to make room, and within a day or so of new Moon it’s lost in the glare, as the real one is by day; its place above the horizon is real (compass along the bottom). Rise and set are when the Moon's top edge meets a flat horizon. Positions follow Meeus' Astronomical Algorithms, good to a few minutes. The seas, mountain ranges and about eighty big craters are traced from lunar charts; their heights are stretched about three times so the shadows show where day meets night. Eclipses of the Moon use Earth’s shadow from Meeus’ chapter 54, a little enlarged for our air, as almanacs do; eclipses of the Sun are worked out for the exact spot you pick, with the Moon’s parallax, and come out within about a minute of the published times. build v1.12.1 · 2026-09-29
